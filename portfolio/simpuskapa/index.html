Project's Overview

SIMPUSKAPA is a Management Information System for PUSKAPA (Pusat Kajian dan Advokasi Perlindungan dan Kualitas Hidup Anak), a think tank group at Universitas Indonesia.

The project was initiated because PUSKAPA's existing information was not yet sufficiently integrated and centralized, making it difficult to retrieve historical information across a project lifecycle. The proposed system was intended to improve the existing intranet and establish a more integrated information and management system.

The system brings together operational information, project management, budget needs, requisitions, approvals, procurement, personnel, travel, finance, and knowledge management into connected workflows.

Project Process

The project involved several interconnected processes, where decisions made at one stage could influence subsequent stages. Rather than designing each feature independently, the work focused on understanding how different activities, data, requests, approvals, and financial processes could work together as a coherent system.

The project was carried out through four main phases:

1. Analysis

The first phase focused on understanding the existing system, operational requirements, and the needs of the people who would use the system.

This included reviewing relevant documentation, studying existing processes, and conducting interviews with stakeholders and relevant partners when required. The main objective was to understand the relationships between processes and identify what the system needed to support.

This phase established the foundation for translating operational requirements into a structured digital product, rather than approaching the project as a collection of individual screens or features.

2. Information Architecture & System Flow

The second phase focused on translating the findings from the analysis into the structure and flow of the system.

This included defining the information architecture, relationships between modules, user flows, and the movement of information across different stages of a process. Particular attention was given to ensuring that related processes could remain connected as users moved from one activity to another.

The resulting structure became the foundation for designing the system's navigation, workflows, forms, dashboards, and other interfaces.

3. Implementation

The implementation phase involved translating the defined system structure into a working digital product.

The design was developed in collaboration with the System Analyst and development team, allowing requirements, system logic, and interface design to be considered together. During this phase, the design was also reviewed against implementation constraints and adjusted where necessary.

This collaboration helped maintain alignment between the intended user experience and the system being developed.

4. Testing, Iteration & Collaboration

The final phase focused on validating the system through different user and process scenarios.

Testing was used to review whether the designed flows, information, states, and interactions worked consistently across connected processes. Findings from testing and implementation reviews were documented and used to refine the system.

This phase was iterative, with continuous collaboration between design, analysis, development, and stakeholders. Rather than treating testing as a final step, it became part of the ongoing process of improving and aligning the product.

Results

The resulting system supports several teams involved in PUSKAPA's operational, financial, and program activities:

Team Role in the System
Operations Supports procurement, asset management, and operational processes
Finance Manages project-related funds, payments, tax, and financial processes
Program Supports research, field activities, advocacy, and dissemination
Management Reviews and approves projects, budgets, and requests

The system brings together key areas of organizational management through modules covering Human Resources, Finance, Projects, Budgets, and Procurement.

Beyond providing a centralized place to manage these activities, the system was designed to make processes easier to follow and information easier to trace. Users can track the progression of requests and approvals, while related documents and supporting artifacts remain connected to their respective processes.

The result is a more connected management system that helps users navigate operational processes, follow approval progress, and trace the information and documentation associated with each request.

Learning & Takeaway

From a product perspective, the project resulted in more than a collection of administrative modules. It established a connected workflow in which information, requests, approvals, and supporting artifacts could be managed as part of the same system. This required aligning the user experience with the underlying operational flow and collaborating across design, analysis, development, and stakeholder review.
